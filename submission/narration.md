# Doomscroll Autopsy: demo video narration

Voice: ElevenLabs Matilda (eleven_v3). Runtime 1:43.

It's eleven a.m. You open one tab. Two hours later, you're still scrolling, and the best focus of your day is gone.

Willpower can't beat a feed built by thousands of engineers. Screen-time apps just report the damage. Doomscroll Autopsy, a Chrome extension, sees the rabbit hole coming, and steps in before it costs you your deep work.

First, it reads what's on your screen. Every post, video or page becomes a vector: a list of numbers that captures its meaning. That's EmbeddingGemma, running on your own GPU. Cooking videos land close together. Python docs land far away.

Those points form a map of your attention. Deep work. Shallow work. And the drains that eat your day. Built from what you actually look at, not banned sites.

Then it watches the signals that come before a rabbit hole: how fast you scroll, where you just came from, how long you've stayed, whether you just left deep work. When they add up, you get a calm nudge, before it becomes a lost afternoon. Ignore it, and it escalates. A banner. Then a blur. Then a thirty-second breathing pause.

Each night, a fine-tuned Gemma 3 model reads your day and writes your autopsy. First, when you went deep. Then, what broke it. Finally, what to change tomorrow.

It all runs inside your browser. No account. No cloud. Nothing you browse ever leaves your laptop. Take back your focus. Doomscroll Autopsy. Protect the work that pays you.
